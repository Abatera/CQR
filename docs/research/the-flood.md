# Research: the Flood of Noah

Research done on 2026-11-25. It is the evidence base for the entry "The Flood of Noah" (`content/events/the-flood.yaml`) and three related entries. All are **drafts** for the owner to review. See also [prophets-chronology.md](prophets-chronology.md) for how Noah is dated.

## Method and its limits

- **Read directly:**
  - **Tafsir** on the King Saud University tafsir site (quran.ksu.edu.sa): Ibn Kathir on 11:40, 11:44 and 37:77; al-Tabari on 11:44 and 37:77, with his narration numbers; al-Qurtubi on 11:44.
  - **Fatwas:** Ibn Baz's fatwa 23538 (binbaz.org.sa); Muhammad 'Abduh's fatwa of 1900 at Dar al-Ifta al-Misriyya; islamqa.info answers 327751 and 332311, which quote Ibn Kathir, Ibn 'Ashur, Ibn Khaldun, Ibn Hajar, Ibn 'Atiyya and al-Shinqiti with volume and page.
  - **Ancient texts:** the Sumerian King List and the Sumerian Flood Story in the Electronic Text Corpus of Sumerian Literature (Oxford); Josephus, *Antiquities* 1.3 (Whiston translation); Genesis 7–9 (World English Bible).
  - **Archaeology and geology:** S. N. Kramer, "Reflections on the Mesopotamian Flood" (*Expedition* 9:4, 1967, Penn Museum); L. Lippsett, "Noah's Not-so-big Flood" (*Oceanus* 47:2, 2009, Woods Hole); a 2002 Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute news release.
- **Read only through another work:** Ibn 'Ashur, Ibn Hajar, al-Shinqiti, Ibn Khaldun, and Ibn Kathir's tafsir at 7/22, all as quoted by islamqa. The sources' notes say so.
- **Not usable:**
  - Ibn Kathir's chapter on Noah in *al-Bidaya*: the Flood details came back only as a machine summary, so none of it was used.
  - Yaqut's *Mu'jam al-Buldan* entry on al-Judi: not reached on Wikisource.
  - The primary geology papers (Ryan et al. 1997; Giosan et al. 2009; Ryan et al. 2003): a binary PDF and a login wall.
  - Mallowan's "Noah's Flood Reconsidered" (*Iraq* 26, 1964), which Kramer follows.

## Did the Flood cover the whole earth?

The scholars hold two broad views, with some who leave the question open:

| View | Who | Where |
|---|---|---|
| It covered the whole earth and all humankind; everyone today descends from Noah's offspring | Ibn Kathir: "the people of the earth drowned, except those in the Ark"; elsewhere "it covered the whole earth, its length and breadth" | Tafsir, 11:44; 7/22 (via islamqa 327751) |
| | Ibn Abbas: "none remained but Noah's offspring"; Qatada likewise | al-Tabari, 37:77 |
| | Ibn 'Ashur: the Flood "covered the whole earth and uprooted all humankind" | *al-Tahrir wa-l-Tanwir* 23/47 (via islamqa 327751) |
| | Ibn Khaldun: it swept away "all the habitation of the earth" | *al-Tarikh* 2/6 (via islamqa 327751) |
| | islamqa 327751: most scholars who discussed it held it was universal | |
| A reconciliation | Ibn Hajar: possibly only Noah's people were on the earth when he was sent | *Fath al-Bari* 1/437 (via islamqa 332311) |
| The question is open | Ibn 'Atiyya: the apparent sense of the verses and hadiths pulls both ways | *al-Muharrar al-Wajiz* 3/168 (via islamqa 332311) |
| | Al-Shinqiti: no sound text shows how far humankind had spread | *Adwa' al-Bayan* 3/482 (via islamqa 332311) |
| | Ibn Baz: the disbelievers were all destroyed, but that the water covered the whole earth "is not necessary", and God knows best | Fatwa 23538 |
| | Muhammad 'Abduh (1900): the People of the Book and the Muslim scholars hold it universal, but no decisive text says so, and most later researchers think it was not; a disputed question, not to be denied on the strength of mere stories | Dar al-Ifta, fatwa no. 4273 |

## Al-Judi and other details

- **Al-Judi:**
  - Mujahid: "a mountain in al-Jazira"; al-Dahhak: "a mountain at Mosul" (al-Tabari, narrations 18197, 18198 and 18203).
  - Al-Tabari himself: "in the region of Mosul or al-Jazira", as it is reported.
  - Al-Qurtubi: "a mountain near Mosul"; it is also said that al-Judi is a name for any mountain.
  - Some, cited by Ibn Kathir, say it is al-Tur.
  - The Torah: "Ararat's mountains" (Genesis 8:4).
  - Josephus: a mountain in Armenia; he quotes Berosus, "the mountain of the Cordyaeans".
- **Where the Ark set out:** Zirr ibn Hubaysh was told it set out from a place in Kufa (Ibn Kathir, 11:44).
- **People on the Ark:** 80 with their women (Ibn Abbas), 72 (Ka'b al-Ahbar), or 10 (Ibn Kathir, 11:40 and 11:44).
- **Time on the Ark:** 150 days (Ibn Abbas, Ibn Kathir 11:44); the Torah also gives 150 days of waters (Genesis 7:24). A report of six months in al-Tabari (18187) has an isnad that its editor calls "doomed on every side", through a narrator he calls a liar.
- **'Ashura:** Ibn Kathir says they came out of the Ark on 10 Muharram, and Qatada and Ibn Jurayj report the same (al-Tabari 18188–18189). Ibn Kathir cites a hadith of Abu Hurayra in the Musnad of Ahmad and grades it "strange by this route, though part of it has support in the Sahih".
- **Noah's sons:** Ibn Kathir (37:77) cites the hadith of Samura that Sam is the father of the Arabs, Ham of the Abyssinians and Yafith of the Rum (al-Tirmidhi, Ahmad). Its grading was not on the page read, so it is not in the entry yet.

## Dating

- **In the app (revised 2026-11-25, D20):** about 3350 BCE, as a *reckoning*: 350 years (Ibn Sa'd) before Noah's death, which is placed ten *qurun* (read as a thousand years) before Abraham, about 3000 BCE. Ibn Ishaq's 340 years give about 3340 BCE.
- **Other figures, counted from Adam:**
  - Josephus: the Flood came "two thousand six hundred and fifty six" years after Adam; the Whiston text gives 1,556 in brackets.
  - Ibn Qutayba: 2,242 years from Adam to the Flood, as quoted by al-Suhari in *al-Ansab* (read on Shamela during the prophets research).
  - The Torah: Noah was 600 years old when the Flood came (Genesis 7:6).
  - None of these is anchored to a calendar year.

## The Mesopotamian flood

- **Texts:**
  - The Sumerian King List ends its first section with "Then the flood swept over": 8 kings in 5 cities before it, then "the kingship had descended from heaven" again at Kish.
  - The Sumerian Flood Story: "seven days and seven nights" of storm and flood; Zi-ud-sura survives in a huge boat and is settled in Dilmun.
  - Kramer dates their composition to about 2100 and about 2000 BCE, with tablets from the early 2nd millennium BCE.
  - The Akkadian account of Utanapishtim survives from Ashurbanipal's library at Nineveh.
- **Excavations:**
  - Woolley at Ur, from 1929: clean water-laid clay, in one pit about eleven feet thick, above Ubaid remains. Kramer dates it to at least 3500 BCE, too early.
  - Kish (Langdon and Watelin) and Shuruppak (Schmidt, 1931): flood layers directly above Jemdet Nasr remains, about 3000 BCE.
  - Kramer, following Mallowan, concludes that the flood was "an actual catastrophic but by no means universal disaster" around 3000 BCE. Whether these deposits are the flood of the texts remains an interpretation.

## The Black Sea flood hypothesis

- **The proposal:** Ryan and Pitman (1997) proposed that the Mediterranean surged over the Bosphorus sill into a Black Sea lake at least 80 m lower, raising it 50–60 m. The idea has since been linked to Noah's flood.
- **The evidence against:**
  - Giosan and colleagues (2009) found the lake only about 30 m lower and a rise of 5–10 m.
  - Abrajano and colleagues (2002) found a gradual reconnection over about 2,000 years.
- **The date** differs between accounts: about 9,400 years ago in Oceanus, 7,500 in the Rensselaer release. Either way it lies thousands of years before the Mesopotamian flood layers and before any reckoning of Noah.
