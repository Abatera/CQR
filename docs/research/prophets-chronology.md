# Research: the chronology of the prophets

Research done on 2026-11-23 for the Prophets lane: the 25 prophets named in the Quran, placed in time from hadith, the reports of the Companions and Successors, and the classical historians. It is the evidence base for the entries in `content/events/`. Every entry is still a **draft** for the owner to review.

## Method and its limits

- **Read directly:** Ibn Kathir's *al-Bidaya wa-l-Nihaya* (Arabic Wikisource transcription, chapter by chapter); Ibn 'Asakir's *Tarikh Dimashq* 1/32 (al-Maktaba al-Shamila), which quotes Ibn Sa'd's *al-Tabaqat* 1/53; Ibn Sa'd 1/35; Sharif Murad's *al-Radd al-Amin* p. 45, which quotes al-Albani and al-Hakim; two fatwa pages that collect the reports with references (islamqa.info answer 20907, islamweb.net fatwa 203734); the Bible (World English Bible), Tacitus (*Annals* 15.44) and Josephus (*Antiquities* 18.5.2).
- **Could not be read:** sunnah.com, dorar.net, Britannica, UNESCO and the British Museum refuse automated access. Hadith numbers and gradings from those sites are therefore cited through the works that quote them, and each such case is marked in the source's `notes`. Volume and page references for al-Hakim, Ibn Hibban, al-Tabarani and al-Qurtubi are as given by the fatwa pages; they still need checking in printed editions.
- **Ibn Kathir's locators** give the volume and chapter title of the Wikisource text, not printed page numbers.
- **The Quran references** in every entry, including those written in the prose, were checked against the Tanzil dataset: each exists, and each passage names the prophet or his people.

## The reports on the periods between the prophets

### 1. The hadith of Abu Umama: the only sound report from the Prophet ﷺ

A man asked whether Adam was a prophet; the Prophet ﷺ said yes, one to whom God spoke. Between Adam and Noah: ten *qurun*. Between Noah and Abraham: ten *qurun*. The messengers: 315.

- **Where:** al-Hakim, *al-Mustadrak* 2/262 and 2/288; Ibn Hibban, *al-Sahih* 14/69; al-Tabarani, *al-Mu'jam al-Kabir* 8/118.
- **Gradings:** al-Hakim: sound on the condition of Muslim, and al-Dhahabi agreed. Ibn Kathir: "on the condition of Muslim". Al-Albani: sound with its supporting narrations; the number 2668 in *al-Silsila al-Sahiha* comes from Dorar and is unchecked. Some of its narrators were criticized.
- **The catch is the word *qarn*.** It can mean a hundred years, but also 40, 70 or 80 years, or a generation: "the people of each age" (*al-Nihaya*), quoted in *al-Radd al-Amin*. Since some people then lived very long lives, ten generations could be far longer than a thousand years. The hadith gives an order and a proportion, not a number of years.

### 2. Ibn Abbas: ten *qurun* between Adam and Noah

"Between Adam and Noah were ten *qurun*, all of them following a law of truth." It is reported from the Companion Ibn Abbas himself, not from the Prophet ﷺ. It is in al-Hakim, who authenticated it, and al-Dhahabi agreed, as al-Albani quotes them.

### 3. Salman al-Farisi: six hundred years between Jesus and Muhammad ﷺ

"The interval (*fatra*) between Jesus and Muhammad ﷺ was six hundred years." This is in *Sahih al-Bukhari*: no. 3948 in the sunnah.com numbering, no. 3732 in another. It is Salman's own statement.

### 4. The report that lists years between all the prophets: probably the hadith you asked about

Al-Hakim's *al-Mustadrak*, in its section "Tawarikh al-Mutaqaddimin min al-Anbiya' wa-l-Mursalin", has a report from Ibn Abbas, raised to the Prophet ﷺ. It gives Adam's age as a thousand years, then years between the prophets after him, ending with six hundred years between Jesus and Muhammad ﷺ.

- **Chain:** Hammad ibn Salama ← Ali ibn Zayd ibn Jud'an ← Yusuf ibn Mihran ← Ibn Abbas.
- **Grading: weak.** Ali ibn Zayd ibn Jud'an is weak (*da'if*: Ibn Hajar, *Taqrib al-Tahdhib*, no. 4734). Of Yusuf ibn Mihran, Ahmad said "not known" and Ibn Hajar "soft in hadith" (*layyin*). Sharif Murad concludes that this chain is weak. Al-Albani, as quoted there, accepts only its first part, which al-Hakim also has by a sound route.
- **Still to check:** I could not read its full wording; sunnah.com and Dorar were blocked. The owner should confirm this is the report they meant and check its text in an edition.

### 5. Ibn Sa'd: the year counts

These are quoted by Ibn 'Asakir from *al-Tabaqat* 1/53:

| Interval | Figure | Chain | Assessment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Adam to Noah; Noah to Abraham; Abraham to Moses | ten *qurun* each, "a *qarn* being a hundred years" | al-Waqidi, from "more than one scholar" | No chain to a Companion. Al-Waqidi is *matruk ma'a si'at 'ilmih*, abandoned despite his learning (Ibn Hajar). |
| Moses to Jesus | 1,900 years, "with no interval without prophets"; al-Qurtubi quotes it as 1,700 | Hisham ibn Muhammad al-Kalbi ← his father ← Abu Salih ← Ibn Abbas | Muhammad ibn al-Sa'ib al-Kalbi is *muttaham bi-l-kadhib*, accused of lying (Ibn Hajar). Very weak. |
| Jesus' birth to the Prophet ﷺ | 569 years, of which 434 without a messenger; Jesus raised at 32½ after 30 months of prophethood | the same al-Kalbi chain | Very weak. |

Other reports collected on the fatwa pages give the interval between Jesus and the Prophet ﷺ as 600 years (Qatada, Muqatil, al-Dahhak), 620 (Wahb ibn Munabbih), 569 (al-Kalbi) and 434 (al-Dahhak).

### Conclusion

Only two reports are sound. The Prophet ﷺ gave the *order* and the *proportion* of the earliest intervals: ten *qurun*, then ten *qurun*. Salman gave six hundred years before the Prophet ﷺ. Every list of exact years between all the prophets rests on weak chains, or on the People of the Book through later historians. Both fatwa pages reach the same view: no fixed total from Adam to the Prophet ﷺ is established, and any figure is an estimate.

## How the Prophets lane uses this

1. **From Abraham onward, years are traditional.** Ibn Kathir reasons that "more than a thousand years" separate Abraham, builder of the Sacred House, from Solomon, builder of Bayt al-Maqdis. With Solomon in the 10th century BCE, that puts Abraham at or before about 2000 BCE, which also agrees with biblical tradition. The ages of Abraham, Ishmael, Isaac, Jacob, Joseph and Moses are the People of the Book's figures as Ibn Kathir reports them, with Genesis cited for comparison. Moses uses the biblical reckoning: the Exodus 480 years before Solomon's fourth year (1 Kings 6:1). David's hundred-year life comes from the hadith of Adam's lifespan, and Solomon's 52 years from Ibn Kathir's sources.
2. **Before Abraham, years are "reckoned" from the ages (revised 2026-11-25, D20).** The anchor is Noah's death, ten *qurun* (read as a thousand years) before Abraham: about 3000 BCE. From there the count goes back through the reported ages: Noah lived 350 years after the Flood (Ibn Sa'd), the 950 years among his people are the Quran's (29:14) and are drawn solid inside his bar, and he was sent at 50 (the first of three views; 350 and 480 are alternatives). He was born 126 years after Adam's death (Ibn Jarir), and Adam lived 960 years (al-Tirmidhi's version of the hadith, as the owner reads it; 1,000 in Ahmad's version and Ibn Kathir; 930 in the Torah). Idris lived through 308 years of Adam's life (Ibn Ishaq), and 365 years in all, the Torah's figure for Enoch. Hud and Salih, for whom no report gives years, keep their windows between Noah and Abraham.

   **Where the 126 and 308 years come from.** Adding the fathers' ages at each son's birth in Genesis 5 puts Enoch's birth in year 622 after Adam's creation and Noah's in year 1056; Adam died in 930. So 930 − 622 = 308 and 1056 − 930 = 126: both figures, transmitted by Ibn Ishaq and Ibn Jarir, are the Torah's arithmetic. In the same count Enoch is taken in year 987, 69 years before Noah is born, so Adam, Idris and Noah do not collide: Adam and Idris overlap by 308 years, and Noah comes after both.
3. **Prophets the reports do not date are drawn in the "unknown" style.** Job, Shu'ayb and Dhu al-Kifl are placed only where Ibn Kathir's order puts them, and the basis says so.
4. **From the 9th century BCE, the Bible gives the frame.** For Elijah, Elisha and Jonah it ties them to kings of Israel (Ahab, Jeroboam II). For John and Jesus, Josephus and Tacitus are cited.
5. **The Prophet Muhammad ﷺ** is dated from the Year of the Elephant (Ibn Ishaq and "the majority", per Ibn Kathir) to his death in 11 AH / 632 CE, aged 63 (al-Bukhari and Muslim, via Ibn Kathir).
