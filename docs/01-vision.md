# Vision

## In one sentence

Link the events the Quran narrates to the history known across civilizations: what was happening elsewhere at the same time, and what followed.

## The name

"Chronotope" is Mikhail Bakhtin's term for how a narrative binds time and space together (from the Greek *chronos*, time, and *topos*, place). The project treats Quranic narratives as events in both time and place, and sets them inside world history.

## Where the idea came from

The owner was watching Christopher Nolan's film *The Odyssey*, which keeps returning to the Late Bronze Age collapse. That led them to read about:

- the collapse itself, around 1200 BCE;
- the slow emergence of the Iron Age over the following centuries;
- the founding of the kingdom of the Children of Israel around 1100–1000 BCE, after Talut (Saul) and David defeated Jalut (Goliath) (Quran 2:246–251);
- how splendid and strong that kingdom was under David and his son Solomon.

Then a verse about David came to mind: "وَأَلَنَّا لَهُ الْحَدِيدَ", "and We made iron soft for him" (34:10). The owner's thought was that David's kingdom rose just as iron was becoming the decisive technology.

### How the idea was refined in planning

Claude pointed out that the strong version, "they had iron when no one else could smelt it", does not hold up. Smelted iron was known in Anatolia and the Near East before 1200 BCE, although it was rare and costly. The owner did not object. The version that can be defended, and is still striking, is about **timing**: David's traditional dates fall in the period when iron became common in the Levant.

The Quran is also more specific than "iron":

- 21:80 and 34:11 speak of making **armor** (coats of mail, with measured links).
- 34:12 links Solomon to **molten copper** ("عَيْنَ الْقِطْرِ"). This connects to copper production at Timna and Faynan that has been dated to the 10th century BCE.

This example is the model for the whole project: a detail from the Quran, its historical context, and a clearly labeled connection between the two. [03-content-principles.md](03-content-principles.md) works through it in full.

## What the project is

- Educational, public and open source.
- Visual and interactive: a timeline, a map and a facts panel.
- About **known history across civilizations**: Egypt, the Levant, Anatolia, Mesopotamia, Arabia, the Aegean and others, side by side with the Quranic events.
- About **consequences** as well as coincidences in time. Showing what an event led to was an explicit goal of the owner.
- Honest about uncertainty: dates are ranges, locations are shown only when known, and debates are shown as debates.

## What the project is not

- Not a "scientific miracles" site. Connections are offered as reflections with a confidence level, not as proofs.
- Not a tafsir. Classical commentary is quoted and cited, not replaced.
- Not about the unseen. The owner decided the map covers **earthly places only**, because the aim is to link to history that is known across civilizations. The Hereafter, Paradise and the heavens are out of scope.

## Audience

The audience was not discussed. Likely readers are curious general readers, students, Muslims interested in history, and anyone interested in the ancient Near East. Confirm this with the owner.
